## Emails and messages

### Request or ask
```text
Write an email to <who, and how I know them> asking <what>, so that <goal>.
Context: <what happened, key facts, dates, numbers>.
The ask: <exactly what I want them to do, and by when>.
Tone: <e.g. friendly but professional>.
Format: under <n> words, with a subject line. End with one clear question or next step.
```

### Follow-up after no reply
```text
Write a short follow-up email to <who>. I wrote on <date> about <topic> and have had no reply.
Restate the ask in one sentence: <ask>. Make it easy to answer: <e.g. offer two meeting times>.
Tone: polite, not pushy. Under 80 words.
```

### Bad news or apology
```text
Write an email to <who> telling them <the news>.
Why it happened: <reason, in plain words>.
What I am doing about it: <fix, offer or next step>.
Tone: <e.g. honest and warm>. Apologise once, without over-explaining or blaming anyone.
Under <n> words.
```

### Cold outreach
```text
Write a first email to <who and their role> at <organisation>.
Why them: <specific reason>. What I offer: <one sentence>. The ask: <e.g. a 15-minute call>.
Tone: direct and human, no sales clichés. Under 120 words. Give 3 subject lines.
```

## Posts

### LinkedIn or social post
```text
Write a <LinkedIn / X / Instagram> post about <topic>.
Audience: <who should care>. The one thing I want them to take away: <point>.
Include: <facts, numbers, a story, a link>.
Voice: first person, <tone>. Match this sample of my writing: <sample, if any>.
Length: <e.g. under 150 words>. <No hashtags / at most 3 hashtags>. No emojis unless I ask.
```

### Announcement
```text
Write an announcement for <channel or audience> that <what is changing>, starting <date>.
What they need to do: <action, or "nothing">. Where to ask questions: <contact>.
Tone: <tone>. Put the change and the date in the first sentence.
```

## Documents

### Summary of notes, a meeting or a document
```text
Summarise <the meeting notes / the attached document> for <who>, who <what they need it for>.
Lead with the decisions and next steps (owner and date for each), then the main points.
Length: <e.g. 5 bullets / half a page>. Leave out <what to skip>.
```

### Report or one-pager
```text
Write a <one-page report / memo> on <topic> for <audience>, to help them <decide or understand what>.
Sections: <e.g. Situation, Options, Recommendation, Next steps>.
Use these facts and figures only: <data>. Mark anything uncertain.
Tone: <tone>. Length: <length>. <Use the <document skill name> skill.>
```

### Slides
```text
Make a <n>-slide deck on <topic> for <audience>, presented <where and how long>.
The message they should leave with: <one sentence>.
Slide content: <key points, data, order>.
Style: <e.g. clean, one idea per slide, few words>. <Use the <slides skill name> skill.>
```

### Cover letter
```text
Write a cover letter for the <role> job at <company>.
Why I fit: <2 or 3 specific achievements, with numbers if I have them>.
Why this company: <specific reason>.
Tone: confident, not boastful. Under <n> words. Do not repeat my CV line by line.
```

## Planning and research

### Plan
```text
Make a plan for <goal>, from <start> to <end or deadline>.
Constraints: <budget, people, time, must-haves>.
Format: <e.g. a day-by-day table / phases with owners and dates>.
Flag any risks or open questions at the end.
```

### Research or comparison
```text
Compare <options> for <purpose>.
What matters most to me: <criteria, in order>. My situation: <context>.
Format: a table, then a recommendation in 3 lines. Say where information may be out of date.
```

## Rewriting

### Polish or change the tone
```text
Rewrite the text below to sound <more professional / friendlier / shorter / clearer>, for <audience>.
Keep the meaning and every fact. Do not add new claims.
<Length: about the same / at most n words.>

<text>
```

### Translate
```text
Translate the text below into <language> for <audience>, keeping a <tone> tone.
Keep names, numbers and formatting. Flag any phrase that has no good equivalent.

<text>
```
